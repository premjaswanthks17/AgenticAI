FINANCIAL GLOSSARY FOR RETAIL INVESTORS
Source: Adapted from SEBI Investor Education and NSE Knowledge Center
Last Updated: 2024

=============================================================================
VALUATION RATIOS
=============================================================================

P/E RATIO (Price-to-Earnings Ratio)
------------------------------------
Definition: The P/E ratio measures how much investors are willing to pay
per rupee of a company's earnings. It is calculated as:
  P/E = Market Price per Share / Earnings per Share (EPS)

Interpretation:
- A high P/E (e.g., above 30) suggests the market expects strong future
  growth, or the stock may be overvalued relative to current earnings.
- A low P/E (e.g., below 15) may indicate undervaluation or that the
  company faces challenges.
- P/E must always be compared against the industry average and historical
  range—not in isolation.
- For Indian IT sector, P/E ratios of 20–35 are historically common.
- For banking sector, P/E is often lower (10–20) due to capital-intensity.

P/B RATIO (Price-to-Book Ratio)
--------------------------------
Definition: P/B = Market Price per Share / Book Value per Share
Book Value = Total Assets − Total Liabilities

Interpretation:
- A P/B below 1 can mean the market values the company below its net assets,
  which may indicate distress or deep value.
- Asset-heavy industries (banking, infrastructure) typically have lower P/B.
- Technology firms often have high P/B because their value lies in intangibles.

EV/EBITDA (Enterprise Value to EBITDA)
----------------------------------------
Definition: EV/EBITDA = Enterprise Value / Earnings Before Interest, Taxes,
Depreciation and Amortisation

- Useful for comparing companies with different capital structures.
- Lower EV/EBITDA generally implies cheaper valuation.
- Indian large-cap benchmark: ~12–18x for manufacturing; ~20–30x for tech.

=============================================================================
PROFITABILITY METRICS
=============================================================================

ROE (Return on Equity)
-----------------------
Definition: ROE = Net Income / Shareholders' Equity × 100

- Measures how efficiently a company uses shareholder funds to generate profit.
- ROE above 15% is generally considered good for Indian large-caps.
- Consistently high ROE (>20%) over 5+ years signals a strong competitive moat.
- Beware of high ROE driven by excessive debt (leverage), not operational excellence.

ROA (Return on Assets)
-----------------------
Definition: ROA = Net Income / Total Assets × 100

- Shows how efficiently the company uses its asset base.
- Above 10% is excellent; below 5% may signal inefficiency or heavy capex.

NET PROFIT MARGIN
-----------------
Definition: Net Profit Margin = Net Profit / Revenue × 100

- Higher margin = more profit retained from each rupee of revenue.
- Indian IT companies typically have margins of 18–25%.
- FMCG companies: 10–18%; Automotive: 5–10%; Banking uses NIM instead.

=============================================================================
DEBT & LIQUIDITY
=============================================================================

DEBT-TO-EQUITY RATIO (D/E)
---------------------------
Definition: D/E = Total Debt / Shareholders' Equity

- Shows the relative proportion of debt and equity used to finance assets.
- D/E below 0.5 is considered conservative.
- D/E above 2.0 in non-financial sectors raises concern about solvency.
- Banks and NBFCs are exempt—their business model requires high leverage.

CURRENT RATIO
-------------
Definition: Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities

- Measures short-term liquidity.
- A ratio above 1.5 is generally comfortable; below 1.0 signals potential
  difficulty meeting short-term obligations.

FREE CASH FLOW (FCF)
--------------------
Definition: FCF = Operating Cash Flow − Capital Expenditure

- Positive and growing FCF is a strong indicator of financial health.
- Companies with high FCF can fund dividends, buybacks or expansion without
  relying on external debt.

=============================================================================
GROWTH METRICS
=============================================================================

REVENUE GROWTH (Year-on-Year)
------------------------------
Consistent revenue growth above GDP growth rate (typically >10% for India)
indicates market-share gains or strong demand.

EPS GROWTH
----------
Earnings per Share growth is watched closely by the market. Sustained
double-digit EPS growth over 5 years usually commands a premium valuation.

=============================================================================
DIVIDEND METRICS
=============================================================================

DIVIDEND YIELD
--------------
Definition: Dividend Yield = Annual Dividend per Share / Market Price × 100

- A yield of 1–3% is typical for Indian blue-chips.
- Very high yield (>6%) may signal a falling stock price rather than
  generosity—examine the reason carefully.

PAYOUT RATIO
------------
Definition: Payout Ratio = Dividends Paid / Net Income × 100

- Low payout ratio (<30%) suggests the company reinvests most profits—common
  in growth companies.
- High payout (>70%) is typical for mature, cash-generative businesses.

=============================================================================
UNDERSTANDING FINANCIAL STATEMENTS
=============================================================================

INCOME STATEMENT (P&L)
-----------------------
Shows revenue, expenses and profit over a period.
Key line items: Revenue → Gross Profit → EBITDA → EBIT → PAT (Profit After Tax)

BALANCE SHEET
-------------
A snapshot of assets, liabilities and equity on a specific date.
Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders' Equity

CASH FLOW STATEMENT
-------------------
Tracks actual cash in-flows and out-flows.
Three sections:
  1. Operating cash flow  – from core business
  2. Investing cash flow  – capex, acquisitions
  3. Financing cash flow  – debt raised/repaid, dividends

RISK CONCEPTS
=============================================================================

BETA (Market Risk)
------------------
Beta measures a stock's sensitivity to broad market movements.
- Beta = 1.0: moves in line with the market (e.g., Nifty 50).
- Beta > 1.0: more volatile than the market (e.g., small-caps, tech startups).
- Beta < 1.0: less volatile (e.g., utilities, FMCG).
- Beta < 0: moves opposite to the market (rare; e.g., gold-linked equities).

VOLATILITY (Standard Deviation)
---------------------------------
Annualised volatility of returns measures the degree of price fluctuation.
- Low volatility (<15% annualised): stable, defensive stocks.
- Medium (15–25%): typical large-cap Indian equities.
- High (>30%): small-caps, cyclical sectors.

CONCENTRATION RISK
------------------
A portfolio heavily weighted in one stock, sector or geography is said to
have high concentration risk. The Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI) is
commonly used to measure concentration:
  HHI = Σ(weight_i²)
- HHI > 0.25: highly concentrated.
- HHI 0.15–0.25: moderately concentrated.
- HHI < 0.15: well-diversified.

CORRELATION & DIVERSIFICATION
------------------------------
Holding assets that are not perfectly correlated reduces overall portfolio
volatility. True diversification means spreading across different sectors,
market caps and asset classes—not just buying multiple stocks in the same sector.

SHARPE RATIO
------------
Sharpe Ratio = (Portfolio Return − Risk-Free Rate) / Portfolio Volatility

- A Sharpe ratio above 1.0 is generally considered good.
- Above 2.0 is excellent; below 0 means the portfolio underperformed risk-free assets.
- In India, the risk-free rate is approximated by the 10-year Government Bond yield
  (~6.5–7% as of 2024).

=============================================================================
SECTOR-SPECIFIC NOTES (INDIAN MARKET)
=============================================================================

IT SECTOR (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL Tech)
- Revenues largely USD-denominated; rupee depreciation benefits exporters.
- Key metric: Revenue in constant currency, EBIT margin, employee headcount growth.
- Typical P/E: 22–32x; ROE: 25–40%.

BANKING & FINANCIAL SERVICES (HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak)
- Use NIM (Net Interest Margin), GNPA (Gross Non-Performing Assets %),
  CASA ratio (Current Account Savings Account), and CAR (Capital Adequacy Ratio).
- High D/E is normal; focus on asset quality instead.

ENERGY & OIL (Reliance Industries, ONGC, IOC)
- Affected by global crude prices, INR/USD, and government pricing policies.
- Monitor refining margins, petchem volumes and new-energy investments.

AUTOMOBILE (Tata Motors, Maruti, M&M, Bajaj Auto)
- Key metrics: Volume growth, EBITDA margin, EV transition capex.
- Cyclical sector—earnings highly sensitive to economic cycles.

FMCG (HUL, Nestle, Dabur, Britannia)
- Defensive sector; relatively stable during downturns.
- Monitor volume growth vs. price-led growth; rural vs. urban split.
