PORTFOLIO RISK MANAGEMENT – CONCEPTS AND FRAMEWORKS
Source: Industry Best Practices, CFA Institute, NSE Academy
Last Updated: 2024

=============================================================================
SECTION 1: UNDERSTANDING PORTFOLIO RISK
=============================================================================

TOTAL RISK DECOMPOSITION
--------------------------
Total Portfolio Risk = Systematic Risk + Unsystematic Risk

1. Systematic Risk (Market Risk / Beta Risk)
   - Affects all securities in the market.
   - Caused by: macroeconomic changes, interest rate moves, inflation,
     geopolitical events, global crises.
   - Cannot be eliminated by diversification.
   - Measured by BETA.

2. Unsystematic Risk (Idiosyncratic / Specific Risk)
   - Unique to a specific company or industry.
   - Caused by: earnings miss, management scandal, product recall, labour strike.
   - Can be significantly reduced by holding 15–20 uncorrelated stocks.

SECTION 2: RISK MEASUREMENT TOOLS
=============================================================================

STANDARD DEVIATION (Volatility)
---------------------------------
Standard deviation of returns measures how much the portfolio's returns
deviate from its mean. Expressed as annualised percentage.

Interpretation Guide (Indian Equities):
  < 12%  → Very low risk (large-cap defensive)
  12–20% → Low to moderate risk (Nifty 50 typical range)
  20–30% → Moderate to high risk (mid-cap)
  > 30%  → High risk (small-cap, cyclical, sectoral bets)

Formula:
  σ_annual = σ_daily × √252   (252 = trading days in a year)

VALUE AT RISK (VaR)
--------------------
VaR estimates the maximum expected loss over a given time horizon at a
specified confidence level.

Example: A 1-day 95% VaR of ₹50,000 means there is a 5% chance that the
portfolio will lose more than ₹50,000 in a single trading day.

Methods: Historical simulation, Parametric (assuming normal distribution),
Monte Carlo simulation.

MAXIMUM DRAWDOWN
-----------------
Maximum peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value over a historical period.
A drawdown of -40% means the portfolio once fell 40% from its peak before recovering.

A lower maximum drawdown (e.g., -15% vs. -40%) indicates a more resilient portfolio.

SECTION 3: DIVERSIFICATION PRINCIPLES
=============================================================================

MODERN PORTFOLIO THEORY (MPT) – Markowitz Framework
-----------------------------------------------------
Harry Markowitz (1952) showed that combining assets with imperfect correlation
reduces portfolio volatility without proportionally reducing expected return.

Key insight: Investors should seek the "efficient frontier" – portfolios that
maximise return for a given level of risk.

CORRELATION COEFFICIENT
------------------------
Ranges from -1.0 (perfect negative correlation) to +1.0 (perfect positive).
- Correlation of +1.0: assets move perfectly together; no diversification benefit.
- Correlation of 0: assets are uncorrelated; maximum diversification benefit.
- Correlation of -1.0: perfect hedge; risk can be eliminated entirely.

In practice, Indian equity sectors show correlation of 0.3–0.7 during normal
markets, and correlations rise sharply (toward 1.0) during market crashes.

SECTOR DIVERSIFICATION (INDIA)
-------------------------------
NSE classifies Indian stocks into the following broad sectors:
  - Financial Services (Banks, NBFCs, Insurance)
  - Information Technology
  - Healthcare / Pharmaceuticals
  - Consumer Goods (FMCG)
  - Energy (Oil & Gas, Power)
  - Automobile & Auto Components
  - Capital Goods / Infrastructure
  - Metals & Mining
  - Real Estate
  - Telecom

A well-diversified portfolio spans at least 5–7 of these sectors.

SECTION 4: PORTFOLIO PERFORMANCE METRICS
=============================================================================

SHARPE RATIO
-------------
Sharpe Ratio = (Portfolio Return − Risk-Free Rate) / Portfolio Standard Deviation

Risk-free rate in India ≈ 10-year Government Bond Yield ≈ 6.5–7.0% (2024).

Interpretation:
  < 0   → Underperforming risk-free investment
  0–1   → Acceptable but suboptimal
  1–2   → Good risk-adjusted performance
  > 2   → Excellent; difficult to sustain long-term

TREYNOR RATIO
-------------
Treynor Ratio = (Portfolio Return − Risk-Free Rate) / Portfolio Beta

Unlike Sharpe, Treynor uses systematic risk (beta) as the denominator.
Useful when comparing portfolios that are part of a larger, diversified fund.

JENSEN'S ALPHA
--------------
Alpha = Portfolio Return − [Risk-Free Rate + Beta × (Market Return − Risk-Free Rate)]

Positive alpha means the portfolio outperformed what its beta would predict.
Negative alpha means underperformance. Active fund managers are judged on alpha.

INFORMATION RATIO
-----------------
IR = (Portfolio Return − Benchmark Return) / Tracking Error

Tracking Error = standard deviation of (portfolio return − benchmark return).
IR > 0.5 is considered good for active fund managers.

SECTION 5: CONCENTRATION RISK AND HHI
=============================================================================

HERFINDAHL-HIRSCHMAN INDEX (HHI) FOR PORTFOLIOS
-------------------------------------------------
HHI = Σ (w_i)²   where w_i is the weight of stock i in the portfolio.

Ranges from 1/N (perfectly equal weights) to 1.0 (100% in one stock).

Thresholds (portfolio context):
  HHI > 0.25  → Highly concentrated; single-stock risk dominates.
  0.15–0.25   → Moderately concentrated; consider rebalancing.
  < 0.15      → Adequately diversified for most retail investors.

Example:
  Portfolio A: [40%, 30%, 30%] → HHI = 0.16² + 0.30² + 0.30² = 0.34 → Concentrated
  Wait: 0.4² + 0.3² + 0.3² = 0.16 + 0.09 + 0.09 = 0.34 → Highly concentrated
  Portfolio B: [20%, 20%, 20%, 20%, 20%] → HHI = 5 × 0.04 = 0.20 → Moderate
  Portfolio C: 10 equal positions → HHI = 0.10 → Well diversified

SECTION 6: RISK MANAGEMENT BEST PRACTICES
=============================================================================

POSITION SIZING RULES
----------------------
  1. No single stock should exceed 10–15% of portfolio for retail investors.
  2. No single sector should exceed 30–35%.
  3. The top 3 positions combined should not exceed 50%.

STOP-LOSS DISCIPLINE
--------------------
Setting a predetermined exit price (stop-loss) limits downside in individual
positions. Common approaches:
  - Fixed percentage stop (e.g., exit if stock falls 15% from entry).
  - Trailing stop (moves up as price rises, protecting gains).
Note: Stop-losses protect capital but may result in selling during temporary dips.

REBALANCING TRIGGERS
--------------------
  - Calendar rebalancing: Quarterly or annually.
  - Threshold rebalancing: When any position drifts more than 5% from target.
  - Event-driven: After major corporate actions or macroeconomic shifts.

LIQUIDITY MANAGEMENT
--------------------
Maintain a cash buffer of 5–10% of the portfolio for:
  - Buying opportunities during market corrections.
  - Emergency withdrawals without forced selling.
  - Transaction costs and taxes.

SECTION 7: BEHAVIOURAL BIASES IN INVESTING
=============================================================================

LOSS AVERSION
-------------
Investors feel the pain of losses roughly twice as strongly as the pleasure
of equivalent gains (Kahneman & Tversky). This leads to holding losing stocks
too long (hoping for recovery) and selling winning stocks too soon.

OVERCONFIDENCE BIAS
-------------------
Retail investors often overestimate their ability to predict stock movements.
Studies show that active retail traders, on average, underperform index funds
after accounting for transaction costs.

HERD MENTALITY
--------------
Following the crowd into popular stocks, especially after strong price rises,
often results in buying at the peak. Remember: "Buying what has risen most
recently" is NOT a strategy.

RECENCY BIAS
------------
Overweighting recent performance (positive or negative) when making forecasts.
A stock that has risen 100% in the past year is not guaranteed to continue rising.

ANCHORING
---------
Fixating on a specific price (e.g., the price you paid) when evaluating
whether to hold or exit. Investment decisions should be forward-looking.

SECTION 8: TAX IMPLICATIONS (INDIA, AS OF 2024)
=============================================================================

SHORT-TERM CAPITAL GAINS (STCG)
--------------------------------
Equity held for less than 12 months: taxed at 15% (flat).

LONG-TERM CAPITAL GAINS (LTCG)
-------------------------------
Equity held for more than 12 months:
  - Gains up to ₹1 lakh per financial year: Exempt.
  - Gains above ₹1 lakh: Taxed at 10% without indexation.

DIVIDEND INCOME
---------------
Dividends are taxable in the hands of the investor as per their income slab
(no longer tax-free as of FY 2020-21).

SECURITIES TRANSACTION TAX (STT)
---------------------------------
Charged on every purchase/sale of equity shares and derivatives.
Equity delivery purchase: 0.1%; Equity delivery sale: 0.1%.
Futures sale: 0.01%; Options sale: 0.05%.
