SEBI INVESTOR EDUCATION – GUIDE TO SAFE INVESTING IN INDIAN MARKETS
Source: Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) Investor Education
Reference: https://investor.sebi.gov.in
Last Updated: 2024

=============================================================================
1. ABOUT SEBI AND ITS ROLE
=============================================================================

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) is the statutory regulator
for the securities and commodity markets in India. Its core mandate is:
  (a) Protecting the interests of investors in securities.
  (b) Promoting the development of the securities market.
  (c) Regulating the securities market.

All listed companies, stock brokers, depositories, mutual funds, and portfolio
managers must be registered with and regulated by SEBI.

=============================================================================
2. HOW INDIAN STOCK MARKETS WORK
=============================================================================

PRIMARY MARKET
--------------
Companies raise fresh capital by issuing shares to the public for the first
time through an Initial Public Offering (IPO) or via Follow-on Public Offers
(FPO) and Rights Issues.

SECONDARY MARKET
----------------
After listing, shares are traded on stock exchanges:
- National Stock Exchange (NSE) – benchmark index: Nifty 50.
- Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) – benchmark index: SENSEX (S&P BSE Sensex, 30 stocks).

TRADING HOURS: 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM IST, Monday–Friday (excluding market holidays).

SETTLEMENT: India follows T+1 settlement (trade date plus 1 working day).

DEPOSITORIES: NSDL (National Securities Depository Ltd) and CDSL
  (Central Depository Services Ltd) hold shares in dematerialised (demat) form.

3. TYPES OF SECURITIES
=============================================================================

EQUITY SHARES
-------------
Represent ownership in a company. Equity shareholders bear the highest risk
but also enjoy the highest potential return through capital appreciation and
dividends. Equity investors rank last in case of insolvency.

PREFERENCE SHARES
-----------------
Carry a fixed dividend and rank above equity in a liquidation, but usually
lack voting rights. Hybrid between equity and debt.

BONDS / DEBENTURES
------------------
Debt instruments where the company promises to repay principal with interest.
Government bonds (G-Secs, T-Bills) carry sovereign guarantee; corporate bonds
carry credit risk (rated by CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, Fitch India).

MUTUAL FUNDS
------------
Pool money from many investors and invest in a diversified portfolio managed
by a professional fund manager. SEBI regulates all mutual funds in India.
Types: Equity funds, Debt funds, Hybrid funds, Index funds, ETFs.

4. KEY RISKS EVERY INVESTOR MUST UNDERSTAND
=============================================================================

MARKET RISK (Systematic Risk)
------------------------------
The risk that the entire market declines due to macroeconomic factors—
interest rate hikes, inflation, geopolitical events, pandemic, etc.
Cannot be eliminated through diversification; measured by Beta.

COMPANY-SPECIFIC RISK (Unsystematic Risk)
------------------------------------------
Risk specific to an individual company—management fraud, product failure,
regulatory penalty. Can be reduced by diversifying across companies and sectors.

LIQUIDITY RISK
--------------
The risk that you may not be able to sell a security quickly at a fair price.
Small-cap and mid-cap stocks may have lower trading volumes.

CONCENTRATION RISK
------------------
Over-investing in one stock, sector or geography magnifies losses if that
position underperforms. SEBI advises retail investors to maintain diversified
portfolios across multiple sectors and asset classes.

INFLATION RISK
--------------
Returns must beat inflation to create real wealth. Fixed deposits that earn
less than inflation erode purchasing power.

CURRENCY RISK
-------------
Relevant for investors in companies with significant USD revenues or USD costs,
or for direct foreign investment.

5. FUNDAMENTAL ANALYSIS – SEBI'S RECOMMENDED APPROACH
=============================================================================

SEBI encourages retail investors to perform fundamental analysis before
investing in any company. Key steps:

Step 1: Understand the Business
- What does the company do? Who are its customers?
- What is its competitive advantage (moat)?

Step 2: Read the Annual Report
- Board of Directors' Report, Management Discussion & Analysis (MD&A),
  Auditor's Report, and Financial Statements.
- Download free from BSE (bseindia.com) or NSE (nseindia.com).

Step 3: Analyse Financial Ratios
- Profitability: ROE, ROA, EBITDA margin.
- Valuation: P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA.
- Debt: D/E ratio, interest coverage, current ratio.
- Cash generation: Free Cash Flow.

Step 4: Compare with Peers
- Never evaluate a company in isolation; compare against industry peers
  and historical averages.

Step 5: Check Corporate Governance
- Independent directors, promoter pledging of shares, related-party transactions.
- Companies with high promoter pledging carry elevated risk.

6. PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION PRINCIPLES
=============================================================================

ASSET ALLOCATION
----------------
Allocate investments across asset classes (equity, debt, gold, real estate)
based on your risk tolerance, investment horizon and financial goals.

Rule of thumb for equity allocation: (100 − Age)% in equity.
Example: A 30-year-old investor may allocate 70% to equity.

DIVERSIFICATION
---------------
Spread across:
  - At least 8–15 stocks to eliminate unsystematic risk.
  - Multiple sectors (IT, banking, FMCG, pharma, infrastructure).
  - Different market caps (large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap).

REBALANCING
-----------
Periodically (at least annually) rebalance the portfolio to the original
target allocation if any position has drifted significantly.

SYSTEMATIC INVESTMENT PLAN (SIP)
---------------------------------
Invest a fixed amount regularly (monthly) rather than timing the market.
SIP in mutual funds/ETFs benefits from rupee-cost averaging and compounding.

7. INVESTOR RIGHTS
=============================================================================

Every retail investor registered with a SEBI-regulated broker has the right to:
  1. Receive a Contract Note for every trade executed.
  2. Access holding statements from depositories (NSDL/CDSL).
  3. File a complaint on SEBI's SCORES portal (scores.sebi.gov.in).
  4. Participate in shareholder meetings (AGM/EGM) and vote on resolutions.
  5. Receive dividends, bonus shares and rights in a timely manner.

=============================================================================
8. RED FLAGS – WARNING SIGNS FOR INVESTORS
=============================================================================

  ⚠ Unsolicited tips via WhatsApp, Telegram, SMS or social media.
  ⚠ Guaranteed returns above bank FD rates.
  ⚠ Requests to transfer funds to unregistered entities.
  ⚠ Promoter stake continuously declining (insider selling).
  ⚠ High and increasing promoter share pledging.
  ⚠ Auditor qualifications or resignation without clear explanation.
  ⚠ Persistent negative operating cash flow despite reported profit.
  ⚠ Frequent change of auditors or CFO.
  ⚠ Related-party transactions of unusual size or frequency.

9. COMMONLY USED INDIAN MARKET INDICES
=============================================================================

NIFTY 50 (NSE)
--------------
Tracks 50 large-cap stocks across 13 sectors. Widely used as the benchmark
for Indian equity performance. Managed by NSE Indices Ltd.
Ticker on NSE: ^NSEI

BSE SENSEX
----------
Tracks 30 large and financially sound companies listed on BSE.
One of the oldest indices in Asia (since 1986). Ticker: ^BSESN

NIFTY NEXT 50: The next 50 stocks after Nifty 50 by free-float market cap.
NIFTY MIDCAP 150: Mid-cap segment benchmark.
NIFTY SMALLCAP 250: Small-cap segment benchmark.
NIFTY BANK: Tracks the most liquid banking stocks.
NIFTY IT: Tracks IT sector performance.

10. HOW TO READ AN NSE/BSE TICKER
=============================================================================

Indian stocks are traded on NSE with symbols like:
  - TCS      → Tata Consultancy Services
  - INFY     → Infosys Limited
  - HDFCBANK → HDFC Bank Limited
  - RELIANCE → Reliance Industries Limited
  - TATAMOTORS → Tata Motors Limited

When using financial data tools, append ".NS" for NSE or ".BO" for BSE:
  - TCS.NS, INFY.NS, HDFCBANK.NS, RELIANCE.NS

ADRs (American Depositary Receipts) for Indian companies on NYSE/NASDAQ:
  - INFY (Infosys ADR), WIT (Wipro ADR), HDB (HDFC Bank ADR)
